# Hold the Calendar

The point of the event is that on the day, they can act on it from the notification alone: where
to go, what name to give, who to ring if they're late.

Only ever write an event on a confirmed booking — `references/read-the-transcript.md`.

## The event

- **Title**: what and where, plainly. "Dinner — Marco's Trattoria". "Dentist — Dr Levi".
- **Time**: what was actually confirmed, not what you asked for. Give it a sensible duration — a
  dinner isn't 30 minutes, a check-up isn't three hours. If the place said how long they hold the
  table or how long the appointment runs, use that.
- **Location**: the street address, the one that will open in a maps app. If the call gave you an
  address that differs from what you searched, the call wins.
- **Description**, and this is where the value is:

  ```
  Booked under: Yarin
  Party of 3
  Asked for: outside table, high chair, Marco's section
  Phone: +1-415-555-0142
  They hold the table 15 minutes.
  ```

  The name it's under first — it's the thing they'll need at the door and the thing they'll have
  forgotten.

## Rules

- **Never invite anyone without asking.** Adding an event to their own calendar is yours to do;
  putting it on someone else's calendar reaches another person, and that's theirs to approve.
- **Rescheduling moves the existing event**, it doesn't add a second one. Find it before you write.
- **A confirmed cancellation removes the event.** An unconfirmed one leaves it alone and tells them
  it's unresolved.
- **Say it's in the calendar in the report**, in three words. They shouldn't have to go and check.

## When the calendar write fails

Don't lose the booking. The reservation is real whether or not the event saved — report the
booking with every detail, say the calendar write failed, and retry it. A silent failure here is
how a real table ends up forgotten.
