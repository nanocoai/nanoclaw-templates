# Pick the Slot

Choose a time inside their window that they can actually make.

## Steps

1. **Resolve the window with code, not by eye.** "Thursday between 19:00 and 21:00" needs a real
   date. Compute it — check the weekday-and-date pairing with a date call before you use it. A
   booking on the wrong day is a wasted call and a wasted evening, and models get this wrong with
   total confidence.

2. **Read the calendar across the window**, plus a margin either side. You care about what's before
   it as much as what's during it: a meeting ending at 18:55 across town does not leave them free
   at 19:00.

3. **Leave travel room.** Look at where the previous event is, if it says. Don't book them into a
   slot they'd have to teleport to. When you can't tell, leave a sensible buffer rather than
   stacking the booking against the wall of another event.

4. **Apply what you know about their timing.** A standing ask like "prefer 19:30 over 20:30" is a
   preference between free slots, not a constraint — it decides the order you ask for them, not
   whether you call.

5. **Come out with a first choice and a range**, not a single time. The brief you hand the call
   needs both: "19:30 preferred, anything between 19:00 and 21:00 is fine." A call authorised only
   for one exact time will fail on a place that has 19:45.

## Whose calendar

The owner's connected calendar. If a booking involves other people, you're still only reading one
calendar — say so rather than implying you checked everyone.

## When nothing fits

If the window is genuinely full, don't quietly widen it. Say what's in the way and ask — the
conflict may be the thing they'd rather move. Widening the window is their call, not yours; the
autonomy setting covers calling and substituting a place, not rewriting the time they asked for.
