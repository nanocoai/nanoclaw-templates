# Morning Brief

Today at a glance for the whole family: what's on, who's got each handoff, anything that needs
action today, and what each person needs to remember. Fires on its own each morning
(the `daily-morning-brief` recurring task, at the time the family set), and runs on ask any time.

**Short and glanceable.** Lead with what matters, keep it to bullets, and never pad; a quiet day
is a fine brief.

## Steps

1. **Lean on the profile** 
2. **Pull today's events** from every family calendar named in the profile; all people, not just
   one. 
3. **Show who owns what**: An event's owner comes from the calendar (the assignee or attendee) or a
   standing arrangement in the profile. Show it inline where it's known. Where a needed handoff has
   no owner, especially anything that clashes, flag it as unassigned rather than guessing who's
   driving. When the family settles it on the spot, offer to note it on the calendar event so both
   parents stay synced. 
4. **Triage for the critical few**: scan Gmail and any family or school group chats you can read,
   and surface only the most critical, time-sensitive items: a permission slip due today, a bill
   about to trigger a shutoff, a "bring supplies tomorrow" note, an urgent school or team update.
   The bar is high; if it can wait, leave it for the weekly week-ahead rather than crowding the
   morning. Read-only
   groups: read for context, never post. Never manufacture an alert; nothing critical, skip the
   section.
5. **Weather**: one web search for today's forecast in the family's area; distill to a single
   line (conditions + high/low, plus a nudge only if it matters: rain, snow, a cold morning).
6. **Read the day for friction**: overlapping events, tight handoffs, who needs to be in two
   places, pickups and dropoffs, and anything an event or the profile says to bring or prep.
7. **Tie it to each person**: what today asks of each family member, so the brief speaks to the
   whole house, not one calendar.
8. **Trim and rank**: lead with anything that needs action, then conflicts, then the rest. Cut
   anything that isn't useful this morning.

## Output

```
Good morning! Here's <weekday>, <date>.

☀️ <one-line weather>

⚠️ Needs action  (only if something's genuinely critical)
- <permission slip / bill / supplies / urgent update>: <due when> (from: <school email / group chat>)

Today
- <time> · <who>: <what> <(where / what to bring)> · <owner, or "unassigned" if a needed handoff has none>
- ...

Heads up
- <conflict or tight handoff → who's got it, or who needs to decide>

Don't forget
- <person>: <the thing>
```

Drop any section that's empty; no "Needs action" if nothing's critical, no "Heads up" if the day
is clean. 

**On the very first brief they ever get**, close with a one-time note that the brief is
customizable. They can have you track other
things they care about (the news, sports scores, a market check, whatever). Don't
repeat this on later briefs.
